How the calculation works

Micro: your D3 is the coarse end (97% undersize). D50 is 50% undersize; D94 and D95 are 6% and 5% undersize. The model interpolates cumulative percentage along log particle size between the measured points. It blends these curves by bag weight and reads the resulting diameters; it never averages D-values.

Below the finest measured point, the model assumes a zero-percent endpoint one adjacent log-size interval away. F-series uses the same tail extension above D3. P-series uses measured D0 as the maximum: mixing cannot reduce the maximum size of an included bag. These assumptions are uncertain, especially in the tails. For F1200/F1500 the target D80 is estimated from the curve built from your D94 measurement.

Macro: individual sieve fractions combine by mass. The solver checks the named single-sieve and combination limits in the reference data. It does not treat a single-sieve limit as cumulative retention from the top of the stack.

The solver seeks a blend inside all limits, favoring a safety margin of up to one percentage point at the control boundaries, within available stock. A valid solution may omit one bag. Only compatible materials measured on a consistent basis should be combined; for volume-based reports, equal density is required for mass weighting.

F180 and F220 use their two basic control sieves together for the 40% minimum. Reference: manufacturer distribution chart. Results are calculations against the supplied reference data, not FEPA certification. Micro results always need laboratory confirmation.
